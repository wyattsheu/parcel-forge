# Corrugated carton with creasing flaps

Generated by parcel-forge from run `20260917T150447Z_ext1_carton_pull`. Every number here was measured on this asset,
and every material parameter is an uncalibrated estimate, not a measured board.

## What is in `carton.usda`

- rigid bodies and their masses
- collision geometry
- one articulation root
- four revolute creases with their axes and angle limits
- angular drive stiffness, damping, rest angle and force limit
- joint friction
- gravity and the physics scene

The four creases are revolute joints between the box and each flap, with an angular
force drive. Flap angle: **0 shut, 90 straight up, 180 out like a shelf, 270 folded
right down the outside of the wall**. Limits are -5 to 270 degrees.

## What the USD cannot carry

Opened on its own, this file gives you an articulated box with **elastic** hinges: push
a flap and it springs back to its authored rest angle. Making a fold *permanent* means
moving that rest angle every physics step against a yield rule, and USD has no attribute
for that. Those numbers are in `config.json` and the rule is in `crease_controller/`.

Run `crease_controller/load_in_isaacsim.py` from the Isaac Sim Script Editor to get the
creasing behaviour. Isaac Sim 6.0.1 on the machine this was built on.

## Measured behaviour, with the controller running

- 0.14 N at a flap's outer edge starts it opening, which is the crease yield plus the
  flap's own weight; grabbed halfway up the flap it takes twice that.
- Released above the crease yield it stays open, springing back about 4 degrees.
- Pushed shut again it stays shut.
- Folded right out it reaches 270 and rests at about 269.
- The box stands on the ground and slides at about 0.7 N.

## Not modelled

The panels are rigid. The carton cannot dent, crush, buckle or tear however hard it is
hit; the only thing that deforms is the crease line. Board MD/CD anisotropy is recorded
in `config.json` as material data but is not simulated in this asset.
